Debt Capital Markets (DCM)

Price, structure, rate and place Ethiopian bonds, from T-bills to green bonds and sukuk, with lessons from the 2014 Eurobond

An advanced course on how governments, banks and companies raise money through bonds in Ethiopia, following one fictional bank from its first T-bill bid to a listed five-year ETB 2 billion green bond.

Start the course

0 of 5 modules completed

What you will be able to do

  • Describe the Ethiopian debt market, its issuers, investors, instruments and institutions
  • Price T-bills and coupon bonds, measure duration and build a Birr yield curve from auction data
  • Explain the bond issuance process, key offering terms and ECMA registration requirements
  • Structure and price a corporate or bank bond, including Tier 2 and rating considerations
  • Design green, social, sustainability and sukuk issues aligned with international principles
  • Advise on liability management and debt restructuring, drawing on Ethiopia's Eurobond experience

The case you will follow

Bank A's five-year green bond

Bank A S.C. is a fictional mid-sized Ethiopian commercial bank. Its treasurer, Liya Girma, invests surplus liquidity in T-bills, but the board now wants five-year Birr funding for solar and energy-efficiency loans that short-term deposits cannot safely carry. With the fictional investment bank Entoto Capital S.C. and its associate Dawit Haile, she reads the government curve, learns the terms and process of a bond offer, chooses between senior and Tier 2 and fixed and floating, builds a price of about 15.5% from the curve, obtains a rating and a second-party opinion, and presents the final term sheet for a five-year ETB 2 billion green bond listed on the ESX Fixed Income Market. Ethiopia's 2014 Eurobond runs alongside as a case in pricing, default and restructuring.

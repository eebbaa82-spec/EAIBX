Sector analysis framework for Ethiopian deal flow

Module 01 / 5 · 60 min · Free preview

Use five forces, PESTEL and a close reading of regulation and the state's role to find what really drives value in each Ethiopian sector, then turn it into a sector primer.

In this module

  1. 1.Why one template does not fit every sector
  2. 2.Five forces and PESTEL in an Ethiopian setting
  3. 3.Reading regulation and the state's role for value
  4. 4.Data sources and triangulation in Ethiopia
  5. 5.Adapting the generic template to a sector
  6. 6.The sector primer: five KPIs and three regulatory facts
  7. ✓Module quiz

In this module you will learn to

  • Apply Porter's five forces and PESTEL to an Ethiopian sector and name its dominant value drivers
  • Read a law, directive or policy decision for its effect on revenues, costs, capital and returns
  • Find and triangulate Ethiopian data sources for sector assumptions
  • Adapt a generic corporate model template to a sector's structure
  • Prepare a two-page sector primer with five KPIs and three regulatory facts

  • Erudite Africa course specification: 3.i Sector-Specific Analysis and Modeling, Levels 1 and 2 (October 2026)
  • Erudite Africa Ethiopian-context programme overview and legal register (October 2026)
  • NBE directives as listed in the lessons: SBB/90/2024, SBB/92/2024, SBB/95/2025, NBE/INT/13/2026, FXD/01/2024, FXD/04/2026, ONPS/09/2023 (reported), and the MPC No. 7 press release (July 2026)
  • Ethiopian laws and directives: Investment Proclamation No. 1180/2020, PPP Proclamation No. 1076/2018 and Directives No. 1067/2025 and 242/2013 E.C., SEZ Proclamation No. 1322/2024 and Directive No. 1083/2025, Investment Incentives Directive No. 1064/2025, Excise Directive No. 1007/2024; Proclamations No. 1148/2019, 810/2013 and 678/2010 as reported
  • World Bank: Ethiopia Telecom Market Assessment, Review of Industrial Parks in Ethiopia, Macro Poverty Outlook (April 2026) and Country Economic Memorandum

Examples and companies are fictional. Bank practices are shown as typical Ethiopian practice, not as any one bank's policy. Always apply your own institution's approved policy and the current NBE directives.

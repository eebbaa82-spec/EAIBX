The Ethiopian financial system

Module 01 / 5 · 55 min · Free preview

See how Ethiopian banking grew from a state monopoly to a market open to private and foreign banks, and which institutions sit around the banks today.

In this module

  1. 1.From nationalisation to an open market
  2. 2.The types of bank in Ethiopia today
  3. 3.Beyond banks: the wider financial sector
  4. 4.Who regulates what: four public bodies
  5. 5.Why banks are regulated so closely
  6. 6.The running case: Liya's mapping board
  7. ✓Module quiz

In this module you will learn to

  • Trace the main stages of Ethiopian banking from the 1974 nationalisation to the 2024 opening to foreign banks
  • Distinguish state, private, interest-free and foreign-owned banks
  • Name the non-bank institutions in the sector and who supervises each
  • Explain the roles of the NBE, ECMA, the Financial Intelligence Service and the deposit insurance fund

Built on

  • Erudite Africa Ethiopian Context course specification: 1.a Ethiopian Banking Law and Regulation, Level 1 (October 2026)
  • Erudite Africa Ethiopian Context programme overview and regulatory register (October 2026)
  • National Bank of Ethiopia Proclamation No. 1359/2025 and Banking Business Proclamation No. 1360/2025
  • NBE directives as listed in the lessons: SBB/72/2019, SBB/87/2024 to SBB/92/2024, SBB/93/2025, SBB/94/2025, SBB/95/2025, SBB/99/2026, NBE/INT/13/2026, FXD/01/2024, FCP/01/2020
  • NBE Monetary Policy Committee meeting No. 7 press release (13 July 2026)
  • Capital Market Proclamation No. 1248/2021 and ECMA Directive No. 980/2024

Examples and companies are fictional. Bank practices are shown as typical Ethiopian practice, not as any one bank's policy. Always apply your own institution's approved policy and the current NBE directives.

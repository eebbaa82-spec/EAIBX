Sector investment reports

Where the opportunities are in Ethiopia, what returns are published, and which incentives apply, sector by sector. Every figure comes from a report by an international institution or the Ethiopian government, and the reports are refreshed every month.

  • Ethiopia economy and return benchmarks

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (9 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia's real GDP grew 9.2 percent in FY2024/25, and FDI reached a record reported USD 4.32 billion in FY2025/26, up 8 percent from the previous year. The IMF-supported reform program moved the birr to a market-based exchange rate in July 2024 and introduced an interest-rate-based monetary policy framework, and the National Bank of Ethiopia continued FX liberalization in July 2026 by cutting the FX commission and the export surrender requirement. Average inflation has come down from 26.6 percent in FY2023/24 to an estimated 11.7 percent in FY2025/26, and gross reserves rose from 0.7 to 2.1 months of imports.

  • Agriculture and agro-processing

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (18 sources, for members)

    Coffee export earnings reached a record 3 billion USD in the budget year that ended in July 2026, and agriculture accounted for 32.8 percent of Ethiopia's GDP and 60.1 percent of employment in 2025 (World Bank). The Ethiopian Investment Commission promotes agro-processing in edible oil, juices, animal feed, tomato and poultry, where large shares of demand are currently met by imports, and integrated agro-industrial parks offer ready-made sheds with income and export tax exemptions. FAO-hosted investment cases presented in 2025 and 2026 project internal rates of return of 20.8 to 32 percent for animal feed, ranching, poultry and coffee projects.

  • Energy and renewables

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (7 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia had 9,761 MW of installed generating capacity in 2025, more than 95% of it renewable, and targets up to 14,000 MW by 2030, including the 5,150 MW Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. The government's National Energy Compact calls for at least US$3.06 billion of private investment in 2026 to 2030, with solar, wind and off-grid power open to independent power producers. Tariffs are being raised toward full cost recovery by 2028, and power exports to neighboring countries earned 366 million USD in the first nine months of the current fiscal year.

  • Financial services

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (8 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia's 32 banks grew deposits 40.7 percent to ETB 3.5 trillion in the year to end-June 2025 and earned a return on equity of 27.4 percent, while microfinance institutions posted a 27.5 percent return on equity. The 2025 Banking Business Proclamation opens banking to foreign investors through subsidiaries, branches or stakes in domestic banks, and the Ethiopian Securities Exchange has listed seven companies since January 2025. With insurance penetration at 0.3 percent, premiums growing 43.1 percent, and 135.9 million mobile money accounts, insurance and digital payments offer substantial room to grow.

  • Health and pharmaceuticals

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (9 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia's local pharmaceutical industry is scaling up quickly: the government says local suppliers now cover more than 44 percent of national procurement of drugs and medical equipment, up from about 8 percent five or six years earlier, and it aims to raise the share of locally produced medicines and medical supplies above 50 percent by 2030. The World Bank reports that the national regulator has reached WHO Maturity Level 3, opening new export opportunities, and that the dedicated Kilinto pharmaceutical zone already hosts nearly 30 investors, supported by tax holidays and price preferences for domestic manufacturers. In services, the Ethiopian Investment Commission points to strong demand for hospital capacity, with only 17% of tertiary hospitals private and more than $120 million a year spent by Ethiopians on treatment abroad.

  • ICT and digital economy

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (12 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia opened its telecom market in 2021, and the World Bank reports that private investors committed more than $1 billion in license fees and a further $3 billion in investment while telecom prices fell by around 70 percent. Mobile money accounts grew from 12.2 million in 2020 to 139.5 million in 2025 according to the National Bank of Ethiopia, and with 15 percent active in the last 30 days there is wide room to grow payments, fintech and data services. The government has approved a Digital Ethiopia 2030 strategy, parliament passed a Startup Proclamation in July 2025, and the investment commission promotes software, business process outsourcing and data centers.

    Published returns and yields: 3 figures

  • Logistics and transport

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (12 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia plans to invest over USD 70 billion in transport and logistics infrastructure over the next decade, and the first phase of the Modjo modern logistics hub, with capacity of up to one million 20-foot containers a year, was inaugurated in September 2026. About 95 percent of import-export cargo moves along the Addis-Djibouti corridor and the World Bank estimates logistics costs at nearly a third of GDP, so efficiency gains in truck terminals, dry ports and freight services offer a large opportunity. Ethiopian Airlines carried 897,000 tons of cargo in fiscal year 2025/2026, serving 44 freighter destinations.

  • Manufacturing

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (13 sources, for members)

    Ethiopia's manufacturing sector is gaining momentum: the Ministry of Industry reports export earnings of 607 million USD in the 2018 Ethiopian fiscal year (2025/26), up from 385 million USD before the Made in Ethiopia initiative, and targets 1 billion USD in the year that follows. Manufacturing employment has passed 433,000, industrial capacity utilization stands at 69.2 percent, and about 5.9 billion USD in import substitution was reported over the past fiscal year. Investors can draw on IPDC's 13 serviced parks and economic zones, tax holidays of up to 10 years in industrial parks, and preferential access to markets under Everything But Arms, the AfCFTA and COMESA.

    Published returns and yields: 3 figures

  • Mining and minerals

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (13 sources, for members)

    Mining, led by gold, has become a major source of foreign exchange: the Minister of Mines reported over USD 5.7 billion in mining export earnings in the 2018 Ethiopian fiscal year, with 44 tons of gold generating USD 5.65 billion, and the IMF reports gold export volumes rising from just over 4 metric tons in 2023/24 to nearly 39 metric tons in 2024/25. The government is promoting untapped potash, iron ore, natural gas and gemstone resources and signed US$4.2 billion of iron ore, potash and gold agreements in March 2026. Investors benefit from lower income tax, duty-free import of capital goods, favorable depreciation rates and a one-stop investment service.

  • Real estate, housing and construction

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (15 sources, for members)

    Construction is Ethiopia's largest industrial activity: the National Bank of Ethiopia reports it contributed around 23 percent of GDP and grew 9.0 percent in 2023/24. Urban population is growing by about 4.6 percent a year, and the World Bank estimates the country needs nearly 400,000 new housing units a year. The government has opened residential ownership to foreign nationals, signed affordable-housing PPPs, and is tendering more mixed-use housing PPP sites in Addis Ababa.

  • Tourism and hospitality

    Updated October 16, 2026 · Fully referenced (10 sources, for members)

    Tourism is gaining momentum: the Ministry of Tourism reported more than 1.2 million international tourists and upwards of 2 billion US dollars in revenue over the nine months reported on April 1, 2026, alongside over 30 million domestic travelers. Ethiopian Airlines carried 19.0 million passengers and earned $7.6 billion in the fiscal year to June 2025, and the African Development Bank is lead arranger for nearly $8 billion of the $10 billion Bishoftu airport, a project it describes as set to be Africa's largest airport by 2029. Star-rated hotels, lodges, MICE facilities and tourist transport are open to foreign investors, with income tax exemptions and duty-free import of capital goods available for new enterprises.

How we prepare these reports

We use only reports and official pages published by international institutions, such as the World Bank, IFC, IMF, African Development Bank and UN agencies, and by the Ethiopian government, such as the Ethiopian Investment Commission, the National Bank of Ethiopia and the line ministries. Figures are reproduced as published, with the source and date beside each one.

Where no such source publishes an average return on investment for a sector, the report says so and shows the closest published indicators instead. We do not estimate returns ourselves.

These reports are general information, not investment advice or an offer of securities. Reported returns do not guarantee future results. Check the original sources before you decide.
